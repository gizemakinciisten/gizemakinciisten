## Research Profile

I am an independent researcher working across theoretical physics, computational modelling, and artificial intelligence safety. My research focuses on temporal dynamics, phase relationships, coherence, and the interpretation of internal state changes in complex systems.

My work began with theoretical investigations into quantum dynamics and the proposed role of anti-time interactions in cosmological phenomena. This research subsequently developed into ATL ECHO (Anti-Time Layer Echo), an independent research programme examining mathematical approaches to temporal observation, structural relationships, and computational state analysis.

More recently, I have extended this work into AI safety, particularly the investigation of language-model activation dynamics, source attribution in multi-agent environments, and auditable computational architectures.

My approach combines theoretical model development, computational experimentation, comparative evaluation, and interdisciplinary research.

## AI Safety and Qwen Research

My applied AI safety research investigates whether internal activation dynamics in language models can provide useful evidence for identifying and understanding changes within multi-agent systems.

Using Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, I have developed experimental approaches to controlled source attribution, temporal activation analysis, and multi-agent observation.

A preliminary pilot reported **34 correct source attributions across 40 validation scenarios**, against a 20% random-choice baseline in a five-agent setting.

This result is specific to the evaluated model and experimental conditions. Broader generalization and independent validation remain open research questions.

Related research directions include federated observation, privacy-aware monitoring, collective agent dynamics, and activation patterns preceding observable behavioral changes.

## Auditable Computational Architectures

My current research also investigates mathematical and engineering foundations for auditable AI systems.

This work examines how computational transformations, temporal dependencies, uncertainty estimates, and analytical conclusions can be documented and inspected.

The broader objective is to support safety-oriented analysis in which system-level assessments remain traceable to their underlying observations and computational processes.

These architectures are under active development and should not be interpreted as completed or independently validated safety products.
